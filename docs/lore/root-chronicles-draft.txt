# ROOT CHRONICLES: The Founding of the Isle

*\`**Bedrock book pack, revised to the final biomes. Twenty books, ready to copy in.*

**How to use.** Each book below is split into pages. The marker — 2 — means turn the page. Every page is written to stay under Bedrock's 256 characters (as far as I know, a Bedrock title is limited to 16 characters, so each book lists a short in-game title). Hidden books are marked with a suggested location.

## 1. THE LAST DAYS BEFORE THE PROMISE I

**Game title:** Last Days I\
**Place:** Trunk, Root Chronicles alcove (first on the shelf)

THE LAST DAYS BEFORE THE PROMISE I

Copied by an Elf who was not there, from the testimony of Elves who were.

— 2 —

The Hunt did not begin with a war.

It began with a quiet.

A fairy ring went silent. Then a second. Then a whole grove.

— 3 —

We assumed the fae had moved on.

We are scholars. We assume the mild explanation first.

It is a habit that has killed more of us than any blade.

— 4 —

By the third winter we counted the empty places.

Forty-one rings. Nine groves. A river that had stopped singing.

— 5 —

&#91;Archivist's note: the original count was not forty-one. I have corrected it to forty-one because forty-one matters later. I apologise to the reader.\]

— 6 —

Then the first survivor reached Arcane Grove.

A satyr, burned, carrying a child who was not hers.

She said one thing.

"They are not killing us for what we do."

— 7 —

"They are killing us for what we are."

We asked what she meant.

She said nobody knew. The Hunt had never once said.

— 8 —

We gave her our best room. She slept nine days.

On the tenth morning the bed was made and she was gone.

On the pillow lay one white petal.

Nothing in the Grove had ever bloomed that colour.

## 2. THE LAST DAYS BEFORE THE PROMISE II

**Game title:** Last Days II\

THE LAST DAYS BEFORE THE PROMISE II

As told by a Satyr, third cup in. The Elf who copied it asks that you not quote it at Court.

— 2 —

Right. So.

There were nine hundred of us at the Long Table, and the table was a MILE long, and I'll hear no arguments.

— 3 —

— 4 —

— 5 —

The Hunter did not flinch. It turned and looked at the broken glass, slowly, like a clue.

— 6 —

What the Elf won't write is that afterwards we stopped drinking.

Satyrs. Stopped. Drinking.

Write THAT down, scholar.

— 7 —

— 8 —

But one thing never changes, in any telling, however drunk the teller.

They never touched the child with the lantern.

Every account has the child.

No account has her name.

From the weather diary of a Sylph who flew too high.

Day 3. Clear. The Hunt is following them. It does not use ships.

— 3 —

Not calm. Still.

I do not have a word for the difference.

— 4 —

"Do not follow."

The wind began to push me off course. Gently. Steadily. Away from the fleet.

Day 9. No fleet.

— 7 —

Day 10. Wind from the west, for the first time in my life.

It is carrying me toward land that is on no map.

It is carrying me home.

I have never been there.

## 5. REFUGEES OF ARCANE GROVE

**Game title:** Refugees of AG\
**Place:** Trunk, Root Chronicles alcove

REFUGEES OF ARCANE GROVE

Elven Archives. Written by an Elder, who has regrets.

— 2 —

Arcane Grove took in two thousand refugees in its last winter.

We recorded their names, their trades, their wounds.

We did not record their silence.

— 3 —

They came from everywhere. Satyrs in rags. Gnomes with their instruments on their backs. Fairies too tired to glow. Darklings, in the night, without a sound. Sylphs, who landed on the roofs.

— 4 —

Not one of them was of the same people.

And every night all two thousand dreamed the same dream.

The Elders called it a fever.

— 5 —

&#91;Archivist: I was an Elder. I called it a fever. I would like it entered that I was wrong, and that I knew it, and that I called it a fever anyway.\]

— 6 —

In the dream there was a word.

No one could say it. Everyone could feel its shape.

Like a coin under the tongue.

— 7 —

Then the Hunt reached the Grove's border. We voted to stay.

It was the last vote the Grove ever took.

Nine of us voted to leave. We never saw them again.

That is not what haunts me.

I cannot remember their names.

## 7. DREAMS ACROSS THE WORLD

DREAMS ACROSS THE WORLD

A Fairy song, set down by an Elf who cannot sing and has apologised to the song.

— 3 —

— 5 —

— 7 —

## 8. VOICES BEYOND THE SEA

**Game title:** Voices Sea\
**Place:** HIDDEN. An ocean monument (merfolk perimeter ring), inside a sea-lantern alcove.

VOICES BEYOND THE SEA

Merfolk Tidal Records. Translated from the language of currents. Some loss is expected.

— 2 —

The sea heard it first.

Before the dreams. Before the hunters. Before the first fire went out in the first Sanctuary.

One note, held very long.

— 3 —

We sent our best listeners to the trench.

They came back with their hands over their ears.

They said it was not loud.

They said it was patient.

— 4 —

The note moved. We followed it for a season, through trenches and across the shelves.

It ended where the water went cold, and thin, and wrong. Not an ocean. A threshold.

— 5 —

Before the threshold, it was a note.

Behind it, it was a word.

— 6 —

Above us a fleet of dark ships crossed toward the Isle in perfect silence.

We could not tell if they followed the note.

We could not tell if the note followed them.

— 7 —

Our oldest listener swam ahead and did not return.

Her root is the eighth.

She is still listening.

## 9. THE JOURNEY BEYOND MAPS

**Game title:** Beyond Maps\
**Place:** HIDDEN. Overworld, in a cartography-table chest on a lone ocean island.

THE JOURNEY BEYOND MAPS

Sylph Sky Archives. Written by three navigators who disagree on the heading throughout.

— 2 —

Navigator A: The chart is blank.

Navigator B: The chart is not blank. The chart has been erased.

Navigator C: These are the same thing.

— 3 —

A: Heading north.

B: Heading north.

C: We are not heading north. We are being headed.

— 4 —

Day 3. The stars changed. Not moved. Changed.

We named the new shapes anyway. It is the only honest thing a navigator can do.

— 5 —

Day 5. The sea went flat as glass. Our wakes did not spread.

A: They are not wakes.

B: They are scars.

C: We are being written on.

— 6 —

Day 8. Fog. In the fog, a low shore. Pale purple. Red caps along the sand.

B: There is no harbour.

A: Then why does the wind keep pushing us in?

— 7 —

The wind pushed the whole fleet onto the beach. Behind us the sea shut like a door.

Navigator C wrote one word under the coastline.

"Home."

She had never seen it.

None of us disagreed.

## 10. FIRST SIGHT OF YDRASIL

**Game title:** First Sight\
**Place:** Canopy, Root Chronicles, near the entrance (a good first hook)

FIRST SIGHT OF YDRASIL

Written by the first Elf ashore. She was not the first to arrive. She was the first to look up.

— 2 —

We came ashore at dawn, and the first thing we saw was nothing.

A purple plain. Red mushrooms scattered like coins.

— 3 —

Then I looked up.

A Tree. Black bark, amethyst in its veins. Taller than a watchtower.

Not one leaf on it. In a land that grew only mushrooms.

— 4 —

I assumed it was dead.

The Dwarves, already at its base with their ears to the bark, said nothing.

I have since learned that Dwarves lie by omission.

— 5 —

Beneath it sat a villager in a green robe, eating a flower.

He had been there, he told me later, for some time.

He did not say how long.

— 6 —

&#91;Archivist: This is the only account in which the Tree is bare. Every later account describes it in bloom. I believe the author. I do not believe the later accounts. I cannot say why.\]

— 7 —

I asked the villager what the Tree was called.

He looked at me for a very long time.

"Hrmm."

And the Tree, very softly, hummed back.

## 12. THE DEEPDELVERS

**Place:** Dwarven Deep Records, upper roots

THE DEEPDELVERS

Dwarven Deep Records. Elven annotations in brackets. Dwarven corrections in the margins, where they have won.

— 2 —

They were not an order at first.

They were eleven stubborn dwarves with nowhere better to be.

Garrun, the eldest, called them the ones who go down.

Others shortened it. Deepdelvers.

— 3 —

By the end of the first year there were four hundred.

Above the tunnel mouth, in chalk, was a rule.

— 4 —

&#91;Margin: "Elf thinks we are lazy. We are careful."\]

— 6 —

Neatly folded.

Corrected.

— 8 —

The Elf asked Garrun what the forges and the armory were for.

"Prepare," he said.

Then, more slowly, he pointed up.

THE SILENT CHAMBER

Elven account. Dwarven marginalia. Gnomish footnotes, of which the Gnomes are very proud.

It was not quiet. Quiet is the absence of noise.

This was the absence of return.

A dwarf shouted at the entrance and heard his voice leave him and not come back.

— 3 —

The Elves, who value sound above all things, found it unbearable.

One began humming to test it. She stopped when she realised she could no longer hear herself.

— 4 —

## 15. THE FIRST LOCK

**Game title:** First Lock\
**Place:** Deep roots, near the pedestals. The second-to-last Chronicle a player should find.

THE FIRST LOCK

Recorded from the testimony of Torvik the Deepdelver, who remembers none of it.

— 2 —

It was a dwarf who tried it. Of course it was.

Torvik had dreamed the same word every night for a year. He stood before the Monolith and said it. Nobody told him to. Nobody could have stopped him.

— 3 —

The word left his mouth. It did not echo. It did not carry.

It was simply taken.

And somewhere in the stone, with a sound like the world's slowest door, something shut.

— 4 —

The hum eased. A draft ceased that no one had noticed until it stopped.

The Elves wrote that it smelled of nothing they knew. Cold, thin air. Air from a place with no sun.

— 5 —

"What did you say?" a Gnome asked.

Torvik opened his mouth. And found, with a slow terrible calm, that he could not remember.

Not the meaning. Not the sound. Not the shape.

— 6 —

"I spoke it," he said.

"And it is gone."

— 7 —

&#91;Archivist: It was the first. Torvik is the only one who was surprised.\]

Each people, in turn, came to speak its word. Each heard something close. Each forgot.

— 8 —

They called it the First Lock because the Dwarves did not yet know there would be more.

They thought a door had been opened.

It was never opening. It was closing.

What was it that had been open?

## 16. THE KING'S INQUIRY

**Game title:** King's Inquiry\
**Place:** Royal Archives, Trunk

THE KING'S INQUIRY

Court record. The Court insists it was a single, orderly procedure. It was not.

— 2 —

It took five kings. All of them were Hughbert.

The first walked from camp to camp, beach to hill to burrow, and stood before each people in silence.

He asked nothing. He said only: "Hrmm."

— 3 —

And each people, one by one, told him everything.

&#91;Archivist: I have read forty-one such conversations. Nobody was asked a direct question. Everyone confessed anyway. I have stopped trying to reproduce it.\]

— 4 —

&#91;Margin: "We told the king nothing. He just looked at us. We told him everything."\]

The first Hughbert died in a pond. The second continued without asking what had come before. He walked to the next camp as though he already knew.

— 5 —

By the fifth Hughbert the Court had given up explaining this, and the Inquiry was done.

Its findings were short.

— 6 —

One. Nobody called the refugees. They all simply heard it.

Two. No ship has been lost to the Hunt since the First Lock.

Three. Before the Lock, the Hunt followed every vessel. After it, it stopped.

— 7 —

Not retreated. Stopped. As though it had reached something that was no longer a place.

The silence after the reading lasted so long the Elven scribe recorded it as an event.

— 8 —

At last the king, whichever king, stood.

"Hrmm," he said.

And every people on the Isle, and the merfolk listening from the water, understood him at once.

Then we cannot leave.

## 17. THE PROMISE

**Game title:** The Promise\
**Place:** Deep roots, Root Chronicles (the hinge book)

THE PROMISE

There is no single account. This is not an accident.

— 2 —

Ask any people on the Isle what was sworn, and each tells something different.

The Elves: to protect. The Dwarves: to hold. The Fairies: to remember. The Sylphs: to watch. The Merfolk: to listen. The Satyrs: to celebrate (misunderstood).

— 3 —

The Gnomes say they swore a number of things and will supply a list.

The Darklings say they swore to keep. Asked what, they declined.

&#91;Archivist: Every account is correct. I have spent a hundred years trying to prove otherwise.\]

— 4 —

&#91;Margin: "Elf has noticed everybody remembers the same Promise differently. Elf thinks this is a problem. We think this is the point."\]

&#91;Withheld.\]

— 5 —

What all sources agree: they gathered in the Silent Chamber, all of them, once.

The Elves, afraid. The Dwarves, not, and said so. The merfolk, who came by the thin root and arrived dripping, and did not leave.

— 6 —

The hum filled their teeth. Each, in their own tongue, spoke the same sentence.

It is not recorded. Every scribe wrote it down and found the page blank.

— 7 —

The Monolith took it.

Not a chain. A weight, settling softly across the shoulders, like a cloak.

They understood what had been asked.

Stay. All of you. Always.

— 8 —

One person said nothing. A plain green robe. A wreath of red mushroom caps, half eaten.

"Hrmm," said Hughbert.

And the Monolith, which had hummed since before the world had a word for sound, went quiet.

Not stopped. Listening.

## 18. THE SHAPING OF THE ISLE

**Game title:** The Shaping\
**Place:** Trunk, Root Chronicles. Revised to the final biomes.

THE SHAPING OF THE ISLE

Compiled by the Elven Archivist from nine accounts. Seven agree. Two argue. They are the two with the best stories.

— 2 —

The Isle was not always kind.

Before the Promise the soil was pale purple-grey, and nothing grew in it but mushrooms.

It looked like a place that had already decided to be empty.

— 3 —

Then the Promise was spoken, and the land answered.

Not gently. It took one winter. Every people recorded it differently, and every people was right.

— 4 —

The Elves did not wake as one people.

Those near the Tree grew crystal in their hair and went to the library. Those in the flats took the spiral farms and the glowing walks.

— 5 —

Those facing the dark took deepslate halls and grew quiet. Those facing the blossoms learned to sing. Those facing the burrows took copper and tools.

&#91;Archivist: Five peoples from one. In one winter. We have never agreed whether it is a gift.\]

— 6 —

In the north-west the toadstools of the Fairies lifted off the ground. Then their houses were the toadstools, stalks and all, hung with dye gardens and leaf bridges.

— 7 —

In the south-east the wind turned and blew only upward. The Sylphs watched the land break into islands and float, vines and waterfalls trailing, while great pale gentle shapes drifted between them.

— 8 —

The Gnomes woke to hollow hills, warm and ticking. The Satyrs found the ground rolling into round-doored mounds under the reeds. The Darklings found black stone rising and did not look surprised.

&#91;Margin: "Darklings not surprised. We find this suspicious. Also correct."\]

— 9 —

The Dwarves took the deep, because the deep took them. Forges first. Then the armory. Then storage deeper than anyone had asked for.

Nobody remarked on the size.

Out in the water, a ring of monuments rose, and the merfolk moved in as if expected.

— 10 —

Last, on the final night, the sea-cliffs rose.

A single sound, like a held breath let go. By dawn the Isle wore a sheer wall to the open sea. No beach. No ladder. No landing.

No ship has seen it since.

— 11 —

Land does not make a people different. It reminds them.

&#91;Archivist: Several sources use that word. I have underlined it twice.\]

One place refused. In the centre, the land curled around a hollow of open water, like a hand closing and stopping.

The harbour stands there now.

## 19. MANY ROOTS, ONE HOME

**Game title:** Many Roots\
**Place:** Satyr Tales shelf

MANY ROOTS, ONE HOME

The Satyr account of the First Feast. The Gnomish account differs by forty guests. The Archivist chose not to resolve this.

— 2 —

The first winter was the hardest.

Seven peoples, the merfolk in the water, one king, on an Isle that had only just decided to be an Isle.

The Elves had the library. The Dwarves had the deep. Neither would say what they wanted of the other.

— 3 —

&#91;Margin: "Elf wanted us to sign something. We signed it. It said nothing. Elf was thrilled."\]

The treaty that held was not a treaty.

It was a meal.

— 4 —

Every people brought what they had.

Mushroom stew from the Elves. Stone-baked bread from the Dwarves. Honey cakes from the Fairies, who dropped half from the air. Smoked meat from the Gnomes, labelled by weight.

— 5 —

Fish and cheap wine from the Satyrs, who brought the most and complained the least. Wind-dried fruit from the Sylphs, who ate theirs first.

Salt, and one black bowl of something nobody asked about, from the Darklings.

— 6 —

They ate in the open grass between territories, with no walls and no guards.

Nothing was decided.

Everything was settled.

— 7 —

Near the end, the king, whichever one, tried the black bowl.

The Darklings went very still.

"Hrmm," said Hughbert.

And he finished it. All of it.

— 8 —

One Darkling stayed and cleaned the bowl. It is the first time the Records show a Darkling cleaning something that was not a secret.

Nobody has learned what was in it.

Hughbert has never been sick.

## 20. THE FIRST BLOOM

**Game title:** First Bloom\
**Place:** Deepest Chronicle shelf, beside the two pedestals

THE FIRST BLOOM

Elven account. The Dwarven account is one line long. It is quoted below.

— 2 —

The Tree had stood bare since the landing, black-barked, veined with amethyst, in a land that grew only mushrooms. Nobody remembers it small.

On the ninety-first day it bloomed.

— 3 —

Cherry blossom on every branch, overnight.

The Elves wept.

The Dwarves, who had known for months, said nothing.

— 4 —

The first petal fell. Slowly. Pink, then silver, then something that caught the light like an End Rod.

It crossed every territory, the harbour, the water, and did not land until it reached one half-eaten crown of mushroom caps.

"Hrmm."

— 5 —

The Court recorded it as an omen. The Elves, as a coincidence. The Dwarves recorded it as: "Petal."

&#91;Archivist: I take the Elven view. I would like it noted that I do not enjoy taking it.\]

— 6 —

Every year since, children of every people learn to catch one blossom. It is good luck.

It is rude to ask what happens to the ones you miss.

— 7 —

Because they do not fall in the grass.

They fall down. Through the soil. Through the roots. Past the library, past the shafts, past the deepest tunnel.

— 8 —

Every year on the night of the Bloom, the Dwarves at the bottom of the world put down their picks and listen.

They say something very small and very patient, below the Monolith, catches every one.

— 9 —

Two pedestals stand at the foot of the roots, where the last petals fall.

One holds a book.

The other waits.

---

## THE TWO PEDESTALS

**Place:** the very bottom of the Tree, just before the roots reach the dwarven areas.

**Pedestal A (holds a book).** Title: The Promise Sworn. Page 1 only: "Seven hands copied the Promise. Seven copies came out blank. This is the eighth. It is blank too." Put a lectern or protected frame so it cannot be taken.

**Pedestal B (empty).** Sign (Bedrock signs are short): FOR THE / ONE WHO / REMEMBERS. Nothing explains it, anywhere. Players will argue about it for weeks.

The pairing fits the lore: the Promise was never recorded, the Monolith went quiet for one person, and one pedestal is waiting for a book that does not exist yet. You can decide later whether it is ever filled.

## NOTES ON THE REVISIONS

- **The Tree is bare at first sight and blooms on day 91.** Earlier drafts had it in bloom on arrival. Now the First Sight account is the odd one out, which the Archivist notices.
- **Hughbert's crown is half-eaten red mushroom caps**, not cherry blossom, because the Tree has not bloomed yet at the Promise. The first petal lands on that crown.
- **Five elf types** appear in The Shaping (crystal, grove, dusk, bloom, tinker). I have not named them in the text, so you can rename them freely.
- **Cliffs rise on the final night of the Shaping**, so the earlier arrival accounts see a low shore, not a wall.
- **Seeded hooks:** a ninth root, the nine Elves left behind, the child with the lantern, the fourth verse, the black bowl, and a number the Archivist has quietly changed to forty-one.
- **Still to revise or write:** Dwarven Deep Records, Elven Archives, Fairy, Darkling, Sylph, Gnomish, Satyr and Merfolk shelves, Royal Archives and Oddities, Harbour, Ruined Portals, Makers, End, Forbidden Archive, and The Lock. The earlier Delving, Door treatise, Harbour and Hughbert books will be revised as part of those shelves.
