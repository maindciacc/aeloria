# Territory Build Briefs

## Purpose

This file translates the current world map into a practical build and planning framework. These are not final canon statements. They are the current build plans for the world as it is being shaped.

## 1. Ydrasil

### Role

The heart of the world. The living center of memory, myth, and promise.

### Core idea

The great black tree, veined with amethyst, stands at the middle of the Isle. It is whole, strange, and alive in a way that cannot be fully explained by ordinary biology.

### Build brief

- Central tree with a dramatic vertical silhouette
- Root system visually strong and clearly important
- Highlight the sense of age, silence, and attention
- Provide clear paths toward the tree but avoid making it too easy to approach
- Add one or two places of reverence nearby, not full ritual structures unless the lore supports them

### Lore value

- The tree is the first landmark of the Isle
- It is tied to memory and the Promise
- It is both a sanctuary and a question

## 2. Arcane Grove

### Role

The Elven refuge and the heart of knowledge, memory, and archive.

### Core idea

Arcane Grove is a place of living learning and deep cultural continuity. It should feel old, composed, and full of knowledge that has already lived through loss.

### Build brief

- Library, scriptorium, observatory, and archive spaces
- Strong magical woodland aesthetic
- Consider layered roots, crystal forms, and careful architecture
- Provide a clear contrast between the forest and the more structured ground of the archive
- Include subtle signs of hidden or protected knowledge

### Lore value

- The Elves preserve memory more than any other people
- The Grove is a refuge and a historical archive
- The first dark signs of the Hunt likely entered the world through this region

## 3. The Human Harbour

### Role

The arrival point and the threshold between old and new worlds.

### Core idea

The only unresolved major map label. It may either be canon, or it may be a practical arrival zone in the modern project. It is best treated as a threshold until the lore catches up.

### Build brief

- Docks, houses, landing routes, and a clear arrival path
- A place for trade, new arrivals, and first impressions
- Distinct from the deeper and more sacred regions of the Isle
- Keep it functionally important but not permanently dominant in the story

### Lore value

- It is the place players see first
- It can serve as a narrative threshold
- It remains an open question and should not be over-committed without lore resolution

## 4. Deeproot

### Role

The living underworld beneath the Isle.

### Core idea

The deep places are older than the visible world; they hold memory, weight, and possibly the answer to the deepest questions of the Isle.

### Build brief

- Large root tunnels and vertical caverns
- Strong dwarven and ancient patterns in stonework
- A feeling of depth, pressure, and hidden purpose
- Add restricted or dangerous routes, but not purely arbitrary labyrinths

### Lore value

- This is where the Dwarves discovered the deep structure of the world
- It is connected to the Promise and to the hidden work beneath the Monolith
- It is the most likely place for a meaningful reveal

## 5. The Silent Chamber

### Role

The place where memory and promise are heard.

### Core idea

This is the most important place in the world, even if it is not the most obvious on the surface. The Monolith is here. The Promise was spoken here. Something changed in the world here.

### Build brief

- A chamber that feels wrong in its silence, not empty in its quiet
- The Monolith should be central and unmistakable
- Add environmental clues that suggest the room is larger or stranger than the plan suggests
- The chamber should feel charged, not decorated

### Lore value

- This is the center of the world’s memory
- It is where the promise was made and where the world became what it is
- It should not be just a location; it should feel like a threshold in reality

## 6. The Ring of Monuments

### Role

The sea-bound watch and the border of old worlds.

### Core idea

The merfolk and the deep sea have a relationship to ancient monuments and to the cold thresholds beyond the Isle.

### Build brief

- Ancient structures on a sea ring or deep-water edge
- A sense of old protection, refusal, and memory
- Visual contrast to the island, especially in tone and atmosphere
- Keep it as a place of wonder, not a generic ruin field

### Lore value

- The Ring is an ancient boundary
- It suggests the Isle is not the whole world, and not the first one to remember

## 7. The territory zones as a whole

Each region should feel distinct from the others while remaining part of the same world. That is how the project preserves both unity and culture.

### The principle

A territory is not just a place. It is a memory structure.

If a player enters a territory and cannot tell what kind of people live there, the project has not yet done its job.
